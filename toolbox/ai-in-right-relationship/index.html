Toolbox · inquiry through practice

AI in Right Relationship?

Working wagers, sources and revision: tools for developing an inquiry across conversations and practical experience.

These tools support a working method: make a provisional understanding explicit, examine its basis, and keep track of what changes. They grew out of Spiralweb’s inquiry into regenerative practice, stewardship and the conditions that support living places.

In this practice, people hold the purpose, judgement and responsibility. AI assists with reading, comparison, formulation and revision; sources, other people and practical experience can challenge the account.

1. Working wagers and your field of inquiry

A working wager makes explicit what you provisionally think, expect or propose, the basis for it, and what could lead you to revise it. It gives you something definite to investigate and return to. A question, observation or practical difficulty can be enough to develop the first formulation.

As related wagers develop, they can form a field of inquiry: connected questions, working understandings, sources and experience. A change in one wager may reveal that another needs revisiting. The purpose is to make those connections available for investigation over time.

Spiralweb’s own working wagers show this method in use. Their subjects include situated knowledge, AI, stewardship, support and institutional conditions. Empirical claims, design proposals and commitments need different kinds of scrutiny; the same test does not fit every wager.

The starter below gives an AI a defined task within this work. Add your question and the material relevant to it. The six tools address particular operations; use them where the inquiry calls for them.

Pocket starter

Help me develop a systematic inquiry from the question and material I bring. Help me formulate a working wager: a provisional claim or working understanding, the basis for it, and what could lead me to revise it. Distinguish observations, interpretations, evidence and proposals. Identify a useful way to investigate the wager, and connect it to related questions as the inquiry develops. Read the relevant material I provide and state what you actually accessed. When I return with new experience or sources, help me examine what changes. Before we finish, draft an updated working note: the current wager, its basis, relevant connections, revisions and open questions. Let me correct it before I save it.

Six tools for inquiry

These are working functions, not a prescribed sequence. A source, observation, disagreement or practical result may make any of them relevant again.

Frame the inquiry

Make the question, its scope and the relevant conditions explicit. The wording can change as the material becomes clearer.

Prompt

Help me frame the question in the material below. Distinguish the question from my provisional answers. Identify the relevant scope, conditions and gaps without adding assumptions as facts.

Assemble the basis

Connect relevant experience, observations and sources. Keep their origin, limits and relation to the question visible.

Prompt

Review the material I provide. Distinguish direct experience, observations, testimony, documentary sources and interpretations. Identify what supports which claim, what is missing and what you actually read.

Form and investigate a wager

State the provisional claim, working understanding or proposal and its basis. Choose a suitable way to examine it.

Prompt

Help me formulate a working wager and identify its kind: an empirical claim, interpretation, design proposal or commitment. What supports it? How could we investigate or critically examine it, and what would justify revising it?

Examine alternatives

Compare other explanations, perspectives and objections. A critical reading should identify what could change the account.

Prompt

Read this wager and its basis fairly. Identify plausible alternatives, contrary evidence and missing perspectives. Explain how we could distinguish between the accounts. Another AI’s agreement does not independently verify a claim.

Revise the understanding

Compare new experience or evidence with the previous wager. Preserve what changed and why, including unresolved disagreement.

Prompt

Compare this new material with the earlier wager and its basis. What remains supported, what needs qualification, and what should change or be retired? Distinguish practical outcomes from changes in understanding. Do not assume that revision is always needed.

Update the field of inquiry

Maintain the current wagers, their connections and relevant material. A working note helps select what to bring into the next conversation.

Prompt

Draft an update I can review and save: the current question and wager, its basis, what changed and why, related wagers and unresolved questions. Preserve relevant earlier formulations and disagreement. Identify which notes and sources should be read when the inquiry resumes.

2. Our digital workbench

How Spiralweb’s texts, tools and public architecture develop through sustained human–AI inquiry.

We use “workbench” for the working arrangement that brings people, sources, conversations, AI systems and developing understanding into relation. It gives an inquiry continuity across many turns. This names our practice; others may already work in similar ways and use other names.

In our practice, the human participant is part of the workbench. Lived experience, intuition, questions and judgement shape what we investigate and what is ready to share. AI helps us retrieve, compare, translate, question and formulate. Sources, critical friends and encounters beyond the screen can change the question and the form it takes.

A notebook, document or folder can hold the first wagers and their sources. With continued use, it can hold a connected body of material: questions, observations, readings, revised formulations and decisions about what to investigate. Keep the relevant material available to read again. A website or contact with Spiralweb is not required. Working wagers in Café Sofia · Arbejd med din undersøgelse · DA.

A living inquiry with a digital memory

Our workbench includes a digital library, earlier conversations, working documents, protocols, versions and the two websites. We select material for the present question, ask AI to revisit earlier work from an updated position, and keep what the comparison changes. A correction can become part of a revised text or tool, available for another turn.

Like sediment settling from moving water, these traces can gradually form ground for later inquiry. They preserve where an idea came from, what challenged it and why it changed. We keep earlier conclusions open to reconsideration. This is what we mean by a spiral: returning to a question with new experience, from a changed position.

A workbench is wider than any one AI context window. Material may be stored and accessible without being read in the current conversation. We distinguish material that is located, partly read, fully read or compared with other sources; verification is a further task. Later sessions may need context reconstructed. Richer context within a conversation does not establish lasting learning or complete memory in the model.

Greater capacity in deeper contact

Our inquiry asks whether digital capacity can remain answerable to the living conditions it concerns. A field question may bring local observation into conversation with soil, water, species, work, research and institutional conditions. We call this pixel precision: making particular connections more discernible while keeping their context. It can make complexity easier to discuss and act within; it can also reveal complications we had overlooked.

Context can help expose a misunderstanding and recover an earlier correction. It can also preserve an error or reinforce our own assumptions. Whether this workbench supports better judgement is a working question. A formulation that feels faithful can help the inquiry move; its claims still need appropriate evidence and criticism. The living field must remain able to contradict our account.

From working understanding to public form

Papers holds the public, versioned inquiry. Spiralweb.earth presents the Association and current field relationships. AnchorPoints hold relational continuity. Within an agreed relationship, the PG Ledger can preserve selected, traceable memory of a bounded field or relationship; Flow Desk helps the Association coordinate agreed work. These have distinct responsibilities. Selected material connects them without making the whole workbench public or turning an AI interpretation into a local decision. Before sharing material, clarify access, correction, pause and ending.

This architecture is being prepared to support stewards and young people in developing practice knowledge through observation, dialogue, action and return. The materials are available; their usefulness in further local settings remains to be learned through use. People decide which knowledge may enter which AI service and what can travel beyond its originating setting. Before using a service with young people, check its current age and consent requirements. Do not enter another person’s private or confidential information without an appropriate basis and an understanding of how that service handles it.

Our next direction: local and connected workbenches

One direction explored here is decentralised arrangements: locally held digital memory, websites or other knowledge spaces, and AI systems working with selected material under locally chosen conditions. A locally running language model could be one component. This is a direction for development, rather than a capability we already provide.

The possibility is a polycentric ripple: learning moving between distinct centres of practice, with context, consent and uncertainty preserved. Knowledge from a place can inform a bioregional conversation and return as questions and possibilities. Each place retains its authority to adapt, question or decline what arrives.

A working note helps resume a particular conversation. The wider workbench holds the inquiry’s material and development across conversations, practical work and different questions. Select what the AI needs to read for the present task.

A small example: watering a nursery

Illustrative dialogue. A steward wants to reduce daily watering work while keeping young plants healthy.

“Could mulch help? I already water every morning, and carrying the water takes time.”

The AI helps ask about the soil, plant stage, available material and who would prepare and carry it. A possible wager emerges: a small mulched patch may hold moisture longer and reduce watering work. An appropriate trial is agreed with the people responsible for the nursery.

After observing the patch, the steward returns: moisture seems to last longer, but collecting mulch has added work for someone else. The question changes: which locally available material could help plants while making the whole arrangement easier to carry?

The practical result concerns the nursery. The learning includes noticing an overlooked cost, revising an assumption and choosing what to investigate next. Those are different things worth remembering.

Between conversation and lived experience

AI helps us compare, imagine, formulate and question. What happens in a place or relationship can change our understanding in ways that dialogue alone cannot settle. In our practice, an inquiry may move between AI dialogue, observing a place, talking with others and returning when something has changed.

We distinguish observations, interpretations, proposals and what has been checked. A situated observation and a question people can investigate may become a starting point for citizen science. Conditions, sources and uncertainty help others understand what the observation can tell them and which decisions it might inform.

What belongs in a conversation depends on the service, the purpose and permission to share the material. A prompt can guide the dialogue; data handling also depends on the provider and local arrangements. Decisions affecting other people, shared resources or safety need the relevant human authority and expertise.

In work we share, we aim to make our understanding and AI’s contribution visible: what we asked, what it offered, what we kept or changed, what we checked and what we can explain. Courses and schools may have their own agreed ways of showing that contribution.

From a personal question to shared work

People can care for the same field through different roles. A grower, teacher, habitat steward or guide may find a shared concern while seeing different parts of it. AI can help make those differences and possible common ground legible. People decide which question to pursue together, what knowledge can be shared and who holds each decision.

Participation also takes time and resources. An agreed project may provide paid learning time, relief cover, connectivity, materials, transport or specialist support. Stewardship economy asks how continuing care and the people carrying it can be properly supported. Rotation can make room for learning while someone carries the work at home.

Our proposed approach to place-based work keeps three readings distinct: the living place, people’s capacity to continue, and the agreements holding shared work. Progress in one cannot cancel harm in another. These three readings can lead to support, revision or pause. The five PG Ledger formats offer light tools for a review, observation, action, decision or resource flow. Each format has a different use; the record can be as light as the work and its responsibilities allow.

Where a monthly rhythm suits the place, a light review can be a useful starting point for active practice. Participants choose the rhythm with regard to local needs, seasons and capacity. The other four formats are used when there is something worth observing, acting on, deciding or tracing financially. An urgent need calls for attention when it arises.

If you want to arrange a shared inquiry, Healthy Roots offers six recurring movements for learning together. A shared question can sit alongside participants’ own wagers and different provisional answers. Choose the tools and rhythm the work can carry. Dansk udgave.

You can use and adapt these public tools independently and choose what to share. Their use creates no automatic relationship with the Association or promise of support.

AI also draws on living resources

AI runs on physical infrastructure. Its use requires electricity, and data-centre cooling can require water. That belongs in this conversation too. The table offers a sense of scale for repeated use over approximately 1½ years.

These are calculated scenarios, not a measurement of Spiralweb’s use or an individual ecological footprint. Actual use can fall outside these figures. An AI response is not a standard unit: length, model, reasoning and other operations change the resource demand.

How to read the figures

Electricity is calculated at 0.3–4 watt-hours per response. These rounded reference points draw on a study of inference and reasoning workloads; they are not universal lower and upper limits.

Water is calculated using a single illustrative factor of 1.1 litres per kWh, approximately derived from the cooling-water and energy figures in Google’s 2025 inference study. This deliberately combines an illustrative water factor with electricity scenarios from another study; their measurement boundaries have not been established as equivalent. The water columns are a sensitivity calculation, not a provider-comparable footprint. They do not describe the full variation between data centres, climates or cooling systems, and exclude water used in generating electricity.

The figures are rounded. They cover estimated operational electricity and direct cooling water; they do not allocate model training, equipment manufacture or personal devices. Image, video and extended agent work require separate estimates. These assumptions give us something concrete to discuss while provider-specific measurements remain necessary for a proper account.

Our use of AI is a choice we have made. The resources it draws on remain part of that choice. We want to keep asking whether the work it supports serves the living places and people we care about, and what amount of use is proportionate to that work.
