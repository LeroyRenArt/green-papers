AI in Right Relationship

Use AI alongside what you know, what you care about and what you can discover through experience. Begin small. Keep the next step your own.

Something already matters to you: growing food, understanding an assignment, caring for a place, making a livelihood, or finding more room for an interest. That is enough to begin.

Start here

  1. Choose something real. A question, experience or practical difficulty you would like to understand better.
  2. Copy the starter below into an AI conversation you choose. Then describe your starting point in your own words.
  3. Try one useful next step. It may involve the AI, a source, a person, an observation or some time away from the screen.
  4. Come back with what happened. Let experience change the question. Save a short note when you finish.

Pocket starter

Help me explore something that matters in my life, work or a place I care about. Begin with what I already do, know or have tried. Ask one question at a time and use my words. Help me choose a useful next step and offer tools when they fit. Read my intention fairly before challenging an assumption. Keep observations, explanations, possibilities and checked evidence distinguishable. Say what you have actually read and what remains uncertain. Help me see when a source, another person, a practical observation or a pause would move the inquiry forward. When I say “For next time”, help me save my question, what changed and my next small step. Begin by asking what matters to me today.

Six tools to choose from

Use the tool that helps with the question in front of you. Return to it after a conversation, observation, correction or pause. The movements can take different paths.

Find your question

Begin with a need, curiosity, responsibility or passion already present in your life. Choose something small enough to explore.

Try this prompt

Help me find a useful question in this situation. Start from what matters to me and what I already do. Ask one question at a time.

Build useful context

Bring a relevant observation, the conditions around it, a constraint and what you still need to understand. Select what belongs in this conversation.

Try this prompt

Help me give you enough context for this question. Distinguish what I observed, what I think it means and what we still need to know.

Explore and test

Compare explanations and make a provisional working wager. Choose a check through a source, observation, conversation or appropriate practical trial.

Try this prompt

What are plausible explanations? Help me express a working wager and choose a small check. What finding would make us change our view?

Invite a critical friend

Ask a person or another AI for a fair reading and a specific challenge. Another AI’s agreement is another reading; verification requires suitable evidence.

Try this prompt

First tell me how you understand my intention. Then identify a useful challenge, a missing perspective and a way to investigate it.

Notice what changed

After an encounter or trial, describe what happened and why your understanding changed. The practical result and the learning result can differ.

Try this prompt

Here is what happened. Help me distinguish the result from what I learned. Which assumption should I keep, revise or retire?

Continue from here

Keep the smallest useful note in a place you control. Bring it to the next conversation, with a relevant source or observation if needed.

Try this prompt

For next time: help me save my question now, what changed and why, and my next small step. Let me correct the note.

Your workbench can travel

Keep a note in a notebook, a text file or another place you control. Start with three lines:

My question now: …
What changed and why: …
My next small step: …

Say Begin to open a question, Continue when you bring a note back, and For next time to prepare the next note. A relevant source, observation or unresolved disagreement can be added when useful. Mark an earlier assumption as revised when experience changes it.

In a new conversation, paste the starter and the note. Confirm what matters now. This is how context is rebuilt across sessions and systems: through selected material you hold and can correct.

A small example: watering a nursery

Illustrative dialogue. A steward wants to reduce daily watering work while keeping young plants healthy.

“Could mulch help? I already water every morning, and carrying the water takes time.”

The AI helps ask about the soil, plant stage, available material and who would prepare and carry it. A possible wager emerges: a small mulched patch may hold moisture longer and reduce watering work. An appropriate trial is agreed with the people responsible for the nursery.

After observing the patch, the steward returns: moisture seems to last longer, but collecting mulch has added work for someone else. The question changes: which locally available material could help plants while making the whole arrangement easier to carry?

The practical result concerns the nursery. The learning includes noticing an overlooked cost, revising an assumption and choosing what to investigate next. Those are different things worth remembering.

Keep the conversation connected to life

A language system can help compare, imagine, formulate and question. A body, relationship or living place brings experiences and consequences that become knowable through encounter. Sometimes the best next movement is to observe the soil, ask another person, rest or return after a season.

Keep what you observed, how you interpret it, what you propose and what you checked separately readable. Useful citizen science begins with a situated observation and a question people can investigate. Record relevant conditions and uncertainty; connect the observation to a decision someone can actually influence.

Choose what information to share according to the service and setting you use. A prompt guides a conversation; the provider and local arrangements determine data handling. For decisions affecting other people, shared resources or safety, bring the relevant authority and expertise into the work.

When sharing learning, make your understanding and AI’s contribution visible: what you asked, what it offered, what you kept or changed, what you checked and what you can now explain. In a course or school, use the requirements agreed in that setting.

From a personal question to shared work

People can care for the same field through different roles. A grower, teacher, habitat steward or guide may find a shared concern while seeing different parts of it. AI can help make those differences and possible common ground legible. People decide which question to pursue together, what knowledge can be shared and who holds each decision.

Participation also takes time and resources. An agreed project may provide paid learning time, relief cover, connectivity, materials, transport or specialist support. Stewardship economy asks how continuing care and the people carrying it can be properly supported. Rotation can make room for learning while someone carries the work at home.

Where useful, look separately at the living place, people’s capacity to continue, and the agreements holding shared work. These three readings can lead to support, revision or pause. The five PG Ledger formats offer light tools for a review, observation, action, decision or resource flow. Select the smallest record that serves the work.
